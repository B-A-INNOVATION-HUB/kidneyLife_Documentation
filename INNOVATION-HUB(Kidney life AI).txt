PART A: DOCUMENTATION ASPECTS
1.	KidneyLife AI’s Relevance to the Theme
The diagnosis of kidney disease depends on the timely reading of CT scans, yet in South Africa’s public hospitals it is often delayed by a shortage of radiologists and unreliable scanning capacity. Although the Fourth Industrial Revolution is widely promoted, few public-sector patients have felt its benefits. KidneyLife AI answers the theme “an AI Solution for Industries” by bringing deep learning into the healthcare industry as a decision-support tool for under-resourced hospitals such as Dr George Mukhari Academic Hospital in Ga-Rankuwa. The solution is trained on 12,446 labelled CT images from the publicly available CT Kidney Dataset and classifies each abdominal CT image as Normal, Cyst, Stone or Tumour, with a confidence score for every prediction. Scans about which the model is uncertain are flagged for radiologist review, so clinicians can attend to urgent patients first while the final diagnosis remains with the medical professional. In this way, the solution reduces diagnostic workload, shortens the time from scan to treatment, makes better use of existing CT equipment, and shows how affordable AI can serve communities that new technology usually leaves behind.

2. Business Objectives
The objectives of KidneyLife AI are to:
Shorten the time between a CT scan and a first classification of the kidney finding to under 10 seconds per image.
Help radiologists order their worklist by flagging scans that the model predicts as Stone or Tumour.
Reduce missed kidney tumours and stones by reaching a high recall for these two classes.
Lower the cost of kidney diagnosis in public hospitals by running on ordinary laptops without specialised hardware.
Keep clinicians in control through visible confidence scores and an automatic referral warning for uncertain predictions.
Build a foundation that can later be extended to other organs, imaging types and hospitals.

3. Business Success Criteria
Overall test accuracy: To achieve at least 90\% accuracy on the unseen test set of 1,871 CT images, as reported by the model evaluation step.

Recall for Tumour and Stone: To achieve at least 90\% recall for each of these two classes, as shown by the per-class recall in the classification report.

Speed of a single prediction: To classify a single CT image and display a confidence score for each diagnosis in less than 10 seconds on a laptop CPU, timed during the live demonstration.

Speed of full test evaluation: To process all 1,871 test images and produce a confusion matrix and classification report in less than 5 minutes on a laptop with 16 GB RAM or less, timed when the report is generated.

Safety of uncertain predictions: To display a referral warning for every prediction below 80\% confidence, checked during single-image testing.

User acceptance: To have at least 80\% of pilot clinicians rate the tool as useful, measured by a questionnaire after a proposed two-week pilot at Dr George Mukhari Academic Hospital.
